Simplified Rugby: Fun Facts & Common Questions

For those new to Rugby, we’ve included some fun facts and common questions.

For those not new to Rugby, please be gracious about the explanations provided. Our goal is to give simple, easy-to-understand overviews.

Fun Facts Scoreboard

Fun Facts & Common Questions

  1. The sport of Rugby is older than American Football and younger than International Football (what we call “Soccer” in America).
    • Bonus Fun Fact: American Football adopted pieces of Rugby and Soccer into their rules when they originated.
  2. What is “15s”?
    • 15s refers to teams playing 15 players vs 15 players.
    • For High School, this version is played in the Spring.
    • Each game roster is maxed at 23 players: 15 starters and 8 finishers.
    • High School games are 60 minutes with two 30 minute halves. (For comparison, all levels above high school play 80 minute games with two 40 minute halves.)
  3. What is “7s”?
    • 7s refers to teams playing 7 players vs 7 players.
    • For High School, this version is played in the Fall.
    • Games are 14 minutes with two 7 minute halves.
  4. Passing –
    • Backwards and parallel only.
    • Forward passing is penalized.
  5. Scoring –
    • Try – 5 points.
      • A player physically touches the ball on the ground in the end zone.
      • If the ball doesn’t touch the ground there is no score.
    • Conversion – 2 points.
      • Between the goal posts and above the middle bar.
      • Think of this as the “kicking points after a Try”.
      • The kicking location is pulled straight back from where the Try occurred, not centered in the middle of the field like American Football.
        • In 15s, the conversion is done from a tee.
        • In 7s, the conversion is drop-kicked.
    • Drop-Kick – 3 points.
      • Between the goal posts and above the middle bar.
      • Occurs during active play and can be from any part of the field.
    • Penalty Goal – 3 points.
      • Between the goal posts and above the middle bar.
      • This is an option available when a penalty is awarded.
        • The non-penalized team can choose Scrum or to kick, aka “Penalty Goal”.
    • Penalty Try – 7 points.
      • Awarded when a player would have scored a try but was prevented from doing so by foul play from the opposing team.
  6. Equipment –
    • Mouth guard. (Get a good one.)
    • Cleats – they must be all plastic or all metal. Cannot be “bi-material.” This is important. Know what you are buying, Keep Rugby safe.
  7. Tackling –
    • Full contact; there is an art to it. 
    • The defender must wrap the ball carrier and contact must be made below the chest and above the knees.
    • Unlike American Football, taking a player down to the ground doesn’t mean that play stops.
      • Tackled player must release the ball and the tackler must release the tackled player.
  8. Scrum –
    • In 15s – 8 players vs 8 players
    • In 7s – 3 players vs 3 players
    • The Hooker position attempts to kick the ball back to their player while the rest of the scrum maintains.
    • Why does the player tap the ball on the top of the Scrum?
      • It’s not required.
      • It’s a signal to the players within the Scrum – they can’t see what is going on.
  9. Ruck –
    • Offensive team attempting to protect and maintain ownership of the ball when a player goes down.
    • There are rules about body placement and at what angle players can enter the ruck. If it is not done correctly, the team is penalized.
  10. Player Numbers (aka Positions)
    • The jersey numbers of a Rugby team have meaning.
    • The following is a simplified explanation of jersey numbers. You can find detailed explanations for the different types of Forwards and Backs on the internet.
      • In 15s – Numbers 1 – 8 are “Forwards”. Numbers 9 – 15 are “Backs”.
      • In 7s – Numbers 1 – 3 are “Forwards”. Number 4 – 7 are “Backs”.
    • What our players wear doesn’t always follow the numbering system due to shirt sizes and/or number of players available.
